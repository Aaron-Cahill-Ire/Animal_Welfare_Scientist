# Research lead: Impactful Giving

Recorded: 2026-09-15

Primary link: https://www.impactfulgiving.in/research

## Current status

Impactful Giving says it will publish a database of the problem areas it has
investigated and the solutions it has considered, one completed piece of work at
a time. As of the date above, the database is marked "Coming soon" and contains
no research records. The organisation explicitly says the database will be a
selective record, not a complete map of giving in India.

## Why it is relevant

Its published approach closely parallels parts of this project's research
funnel:

- separate relatively established interventions that mainly need funding and
  scale from neglected, uncertain problems that need new solutions;
- prioritise problems using scale, neglectedness and tractability;
- map candidate solutions and compare evidence quality, causal-chain strength
  and cost per unit of outcome;
- make uncertainty explicit; and
- turn research into prioritised decisions rather than treating research as the
  endpoint.

This may provide a useful external comparison for the WelfareTech Explorer
funnel and the Animal Welfare Science Agent Commons, particularly the Problem
Mapper, Solution and Prior-Art Scout, Opportunity Prioritisation Agent, and
Evidence and Claim Auditor.

## Questions to explore

1. Does the released database include animal welfare, food systems, agriculture,
   or adjacent technology-enabled interventions?
2. What is the database's record structure, taxonomy, evidence grading, and
   treatment of uncertainty or missing evidence?
3. How does its prioritisation framework operationalise scale, neglectedness,
   tractability, worldview differences, causal-chain risk, and cost per outcome?
4. Which parts could be represented in this project's schemas without importing
   donor-specific assumptions?
5. Could Impactful Giving be a source, comparison case, research partner, or
   route to India-specific problem and solution data?

## Follow-up

- Revisit the research page when the database launches.
- Review the site's detailed essays on prioritising problems, identifying
  solutions, and its research taxonomy.
- Treat any future records as a curated source with stated coverage limits, not
  as proof that unlisted problems or solutions do not exist.
