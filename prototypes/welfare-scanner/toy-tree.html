Welfare Technology Scanner · Toy example

One pathway, three intervention surfaces

Map the conditions required for a welfare-harm pathway, then search each distinct condition for technologies that could break the chain.

Ailment Painful footpad dermatitis
Toy pathway Ammonia-mediated irritation from litter contact
ALL Three conditions are required within this pathway
Necessary condition Damaging ammonia is produced

Change the chemical process in the litter.

↓
Search target Reduce ammonia production
↓
Example technology Urease inhibitor
Necessary condition Footpad contacts the litter

Change the physical interface.

↓
Search target Reduce physical contact
↓
Example technology Low-cost raised surface or barrier
Necessary condition Exposure lasts long enough

Change the duration or recovery interval.

↓
Search target Reduce exposure time
↓
Example technology Dry resting area or surface rotation
✓ Remove any genuinely necessary condition and this particular pathway is blocked.

Teaching example only. A real scanner run would require evidence and human approval before accepting the pathway, its necessary conditions, or any technology candidate. Other pathways to the same ailment may remain open.

How the system produces this tree

Agents turn one welfare problem into node-linked technologies

The causal tree is completed and approved before the full technology searches begin.

O
Workflow Orchestrator

Opens the run, passes structured artifacts between roles, validates their format, records every version, and pauses at both human decisions. It makes no scientific judgements.

Phase 1 · Build the tree

Before searching
  1. 1

    Problem Intake Agent

    Freezes the selected problem record and sets broiler footpad dermatitis as the one-ailment run scope.

  2. 2

    Welfare Evidence Agent

    Returns evidence cards about the proposed pathway, ammonia production, litter contact, exposure, and uncertainty.

  3. 3

    Fault-Tree Builder

    Builds the pathway and necessary-condition structure, asks for missing evidence, and stops when deeper detail would not reveal a new intervention target.

  4. H1

    Human review 1

    Approves, edits, or rejects the complete tree and the exact condition nodes that may be searched.

Phase 2 · Find technologies

Approved nodes only
  1. 4

    Mechanism Translator

    Turns ammonia production, physical contact, and exposure time into separate species-neutral search briefs.

  2. 5–7

    Three Scouts search in parallel

    The Patent, Research, and Product + Trade Scouts find candidates such as urease inhibitors, contact barriers, and surface-management systems.

  3. 8

    Mechanism Match Judge

    Keeps only candidates that act on the exact searched condition under compatible conditions and scale.

  4. 9

    Prior-Use Validator

    Checks whether each candidate has already been used, tested, failed, or is only apparently untested in broiler systems.

  5. 10

    Triage Agent

    Ranks surviving candidates by evidence, expected welfare effect, readiness, cost, scale, adoption barriers, and new-harm risk.

  6. H2

    Human review 2

    Chooses whether to investigate, test, hold, or reject each candidate. No agent recommends autonomous deployment.
